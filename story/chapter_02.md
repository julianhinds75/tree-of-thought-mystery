# Chapter 2 — Scotland Yard

The horse-drawn coach rattled to a stop before the soot-stained façade of Scotland Yard, its tall windows glowering down at London like suspicious eyes. As Paige and Walter stepped out, a young woman brushed past them — raven-haired, red-rimmed eyes, her cheeks still wet. She carried with her a soft cloud of floral perfume, delicate but unmistakably present.
Paige inhaled once.

 “Lily of the valley,” she murmured. “And recently embraced. Come along, Walter.”
Inside, Inspector Francis Jefferies waited with the impatience of a man who believed he had already solved the case and resented anyone who might prove otherwise. His moustache bristled the moment he saw Paige Turner.
“Oh, you,” he said flatly. “I suppose you’re here to enlighten us mere mortals again.”

Paige swept past him without so much as a glance.
 “Only if fate insists, Inspector.”
Walter gave Jefferies an apologetic shrug as he hurried after her.

They reached the cells, where Julian Kent, the gentle giant of Murphy Manor, sat on a narrow bench with his head in his hands. His enormous frame seemed far too large for the cramped space, like a caged bear trying not to break the furniture. He did not look up when Walter spoke his name.

But when Paige said softly,
 “Good afternoon, Mr Kent,”

Julian rose at once — not out of deference, but surprise.
“Beggin’ your pardon, m’lady,” he said quietly. “I thought Mr Bishop had come alone.”

Paige made a dismissive flick of her hand.
 “No need for ceremony. I require only truth. What I will not tolerate is the wasting of my time with foolish lies. You have upset one young lady already — best not make it a habit.”

Julian blinked.
 “How did you—?”
Walter sighed deeply.
 “Here we go…”

Paige folded her arms.
 “The young woman who passed us on our way in had the same floral perfume now clinging to your hands. You were smelling them as we approached, no doubt reliving a tender moment in which she pleaded her case — and you told her, against all reason, that you would stick to your confession.”

Julian’s mouth fell open.
 “We didn’t hold hands… we, uh… we hugged.”

Paige rolled her eyes skyward.
 “Hugged? Of course. There is always something I overlook. Well, no matter.”

She stepped closer to the bars, her gaze sharp enough to cut through stone.
“Now, Mr Kent,” she said.
 “Kindly tell me — in your own words — how you murdered Lord Daniel Murphy of Murphy Manor.”

Julian swallowed hard.
 His lips trembled.
 And for the first time, the entire truth began to push at the seams of the lie he had wrapped himself in.
