# Chapter 4 — Meeting the Vipers

Paige paused at the doorway, expression brightening like a child about to unwrap a puzzle.
“Ah,” she whispered to Walter. “The sweet sound of privilege eating itself alive.”
Walter sighed. “Do try not to enjoy this too much.”
“No promises.”

The moment Paige and Walter stepped into the drawing room, four heads snapped toward them — though only one voice rose above the pandemonium.
“Amanda Murphy,” declared a young woman with a dazzling smile, sweeping forward and nearly bowling Walter over.

“Professor Paige Turner! I cannot believe you’re standing in my home. I’ve read all your cases. The Berlin Affair? Iconic.”
Her voice was noticeably congested. And there was a scent of cinnamon in the air

“Forgive me, I’ve picked up a summer cold. I’ve been bunged up for days — I can barely smell a thing.”
Paige blinked, a little startled but undeniably pleased.
“My word. A fan. How refreshing.”

Behind Amanda, the room was already a battlefield.
“Your hairpins” Paige commented “the design is based off of the flowers from Peru, correct?”
Amanda smiled broadly, “Gosh, you ARE good!”
“Another bloody gift for the spoiled know-it-all” a clearly alcohol influenced voice came from a man in one of the chairs behind her.
“Please forgive my uncle Professor, his idea of exploration is finding the bottom of a bottle” Amanda said smirking.

“Don’t feed her ego,” muttered William from his velvet armchair. His waistcoat was misbuttoned, his cravat wilted, and the smell of expensive cognac hung around him like a small personal cloud.
Amanda shot him a withering glare. “William, please. Go back to… whatever you were drinking.”
“I never stopped,” William slurred.
Adam Murphy — tall, lean, sun-tanned from travel — rose from the chaise with an easy smile. “Professor Turner, Walter, welcome. Forgive the chaos. This family isn’t always like this.”
“Liar,” Rory muttered.

Adam ignored him. “Amanda and I have just returned from Peru. Spectacular country. The colours! The orchids! And my niece here”—he ruffled Amanda’s hair—“can charm the scales off a serpent.”
Paige studied the pair. Amanda beamed. Adam radiated warmth. Both smelled faintly of cinnamon.
Fascinating.


### Sasha Murphy
Sasha stood near the window, arms folded, nose tilted in aristocratic disdain. Her dress was elegant, her jewellery carefully arranged — but the shine was dulled, the polish uneven.
And her shoes.
Scuffed. Worn at the heel. Badly maintained.
Not a woman of means.
Not anymore.

“Forgive my sister,” Adam said mildly. “She often forgets things like manners.”
“And coach fare,” Rory murmured around a smirk.
Sasha’s nostrils flared.
 “Yes, yes — I said I’d settle that later.”
“No, no, Sasha dear. It’s quite fine,” Adam replied kindly.
“Uncle Adam is far too generous,” Amanda said brightly.

Sasha shot her daughter a glare.
 “Amanda, please. Not in front of guests.”
“In front of whom?” William croaked from a chaise. “This woman in a man’s suit, or the skinny chap?”
“Uncle William, please.” Amanda planted a hand on her hip and pointed a warning finger.
“There are many practical reasons for a suit over a dress. For example—”
“I don’t care,” William interrupted, staggering toward a side table to pour himself another drink. He reeked of expensive cognac.
Amanda folded her arms, visibly irritated at being cut off.

Paige offered a polite, razor-thin smile.
Rory stepped forward next — polished boots, perfectly coiffed hair, a handsome smile stretched too tightly across his face. He lit a cigar with a gold lighter as he approached, the rich, spiced smoke curling toward the ceiling.
Paige’s eyes narrowed a fraction.
That scent.

She had smelled that exact cigar once before, in a case involving a stolen French diamond and a pub owner with more enemies than patrons. Completely illegal in Britain, banned due to trade disputes. And yet here it was, burning merrily between Rory’s fingers.
She said nothing.
Not yet.
“Professor,” Rory said with superficial charm, “forgive my siblings. They struggle with decorum. I, however—”
“Oh, do shut up, Rory,” Sasha snapped.
“Sasha,” he drawled, “do tell your daughter not to mingle with the lower class.”


“And you,” Sasha snapped, rounding on him, “you have the audacity to talk about money when you’ve been keeping such… questionable company lately?”
Rory stiffened. “You don’t know what you’re talking about.”
“Oh, I think we do,” William said, slumping sideways. 
“Be quiet, you drunk—”
“Make me, you fraudulent—”
“Boys!” Adam interjected, raising both hands. “Please, for once in your lives, stop.”
They didn’t.

They didn’t even need prompting.
The siblings erupted instantly:
“She’s only here for the inheritance!”
 “You’re one to talk!”
 “If the Will weren’t missing—”
 “Missing? Stolen, you mean!”
 “Oh please, Father loved those damned cats more—”
 “Perhaps if someone hadn’t run up debts—”
 “Oh, come off it, you’re the one who—”
 “Don’t you dare point that finger at me!”
“He’s going to leave it to those bloody cats”

It was chaos, pure and unfiltered — the kind that would make even the calmest magistrate consider early retirement.
Paige quietly observed the storm around her, taking in each detail as if cataloguing specimens:
Paige clapped her hands once.

The room went still.

A small miracle.

“Excellent!” she declared. “You’ve saved me hours of piecing this together. Now, if you would be so kind, I would like to ask each of you a few questions. In an orderly fashion. Let’s start with William shall we”
“I’m bringing my drink” William slurred.
“I wouldn’t have it any other way. Alfred, please escort us to the library”
Alfred nodded and led them out.
