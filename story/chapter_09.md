# Chapter 9 — The Cat’s Eye

The drawing room felt crowded in the way only old houses manage—too many bodies, too much history, and nowhere for truth to hide. The remaining Murphys were scattered like debris from a shipwreck: Rory near the window, smoking nervously; Sasha gripping a handkerchief; William nursing a glass of water; and Adam and Amanda sitting close together on the settee.
Paige stood near the fireplace, hands loosely folded behind her back. The cats, Kal and Màni, lay at her feet, unmoved by the tension.

“Before we begin,” she said mildly, her voice cutting through the low murmur of conversation, “I need to correct a mistake everyone in this room is making.”
William scoffed. Rory rolled his eyes, exhaling a plume of smoke. Amanda smiled politely, looking for all the world like a guest at a boring recital.

“You all believe Lord Murphy died late in the evening, strangled by an angry employee” Paige continued. “This is false…he was poisoned”

The room stilled—not silence, but recalibration.
Sasha spoke first “Poisoned? Don’t be ridiculous, that awful groundskeeper murder my father”, she dabbed her eyes with her handkerchief. “How? Poisoned with what? We all ate and drank the same thing” 
“Not everything, isn’t that right William?” Paige did not look at anyone in particular and spoke before William could answer. “Dinner ended at half past eight. Tempers were frayed. People retreated. Patterns resumed. Alfred escorted Lord Murphy as he always did. Claire’s room was visited as it always was.”

She turned, pacing slowly along the rug.
“The poisoning occurred before most of you began remembering your stories.”
Walter stood by the door, notebook in hand, and felt it then—the shift. Paige wasn’t questioning them anymore. She was rebuilding reality.
 
Paige turned first to Rory.
“Mr. Murphy,” she said briskly, “you are ambitious, impatient, and financially desperate. You paid criminals to forge a Will and to eliminate your father. You attempted to access a safe you could not open. Mr Fingers is skilled, but not that skilled”
Rory stiffened, his face flushing. “Now see here—”

“But,” Paige continued, silencing him with a raised finger, “you are not a murderer. Your plan required paperwork, witnesses, distance and of course help. Poison is not a gambler’s tool—it is too quiet, too slow, too uncertain. You wanted to rewrite the future, not erase the present. And of course, visiting Claire. Oh for very different reasons than your late father, you wanted information. This is why you were able to learn Lord Murphy’s nightly routine and when you would have unfettered access to his study. By the way, Simon Coulson sends his regards and will not be able to offer his services.”

She moved on before he could respond.
“William,” she said, not unkindly. “You drank the cognac. And unknowingly contaminated yourself with the aforementioned poison. Given your addictive personality, you are not capable of planning anything that requires restraint. Try not too worry, it’s my understanding that Peruvian Cat’s seeds, while deadly, require a dose larger than you consumed when you drank your fathers cognac”
William stuttered, clutching his throat “H–how do you know?”

Paige glancing at the cats, “Simple, you’re not dead”
William sagged into his chair, relief and humiliation colliding on his face.
“Sasha,” Paige said next, barely glancing at her. “You have motive, certainly. But you have no access. But you were nowhere near the mechanism of death, and frankly, your footwear suggests you lack the funds to procure rare South American toxins, unlike your brother Adam.”

Sasha opened her mouth to argue, then thought better of it, snapping her jaw shut.
“Adam” Paige said with slightly more warmth than she had shown the others “In your recent trip to Peru, did you spend any time alone or were you and Amanda always together?”

Adam’s jaw clenched “Now hold on Professor, this is outrageous. I know what you’re implying. I would never do anything to hurt my father”
“I believe you” Paige interrupted “You lack any real motive, nor do you have the necessary knowledge, so please, answer my question”
Adam’s shoulders sank “I–I slept most my time there in my room, nasty cough that I couldn’t shake”
“Something you ate, or drank?” Paige asked


“I think it must’ve been something I drank when Amanda and I had dinner, luckily she brought me fluids and eventually I felt better.” Adam said.
The room narrowed.

Paige stopped walking.
“This was not a crime of anger,” she said quietly. “Nor greed. Nor desperation.”

She turned at last, her grey eyes sharp as flint.
“It was a crime of permission.”

Alfred’s fist tightened by the door.
“Lord Murphy allowed very few people close to him. Fewer still could touch him without resistance. Fewer again could act without being noticed.”
She gestured vaguely to the room. “You have all been looking for someone suspicious.”
Her gaze drifted, finally, to Amanda—but only for a heartbeat.
“The killer was not suspicious at all.”

Paige’s tone softened, becoming almost conversational.
“A hug,” she said.
“An unlocked moment.”
“A missing snack.”
“A cold that dulls the senses.”
“Befriending a distraught botanist"
“A book given freely.”
“A lovely hairpin, modelled after a Cat’s eye flower”

Amanda nodded along, attentive, interested, her hands folded neatly in her lap.
Paige took a breath, and then, the tempo changed. The air in the room seemed to vibrate as she accelerated, her mind moving faster than their ability to process.

“You all believe this crime happened late because that’s when chaos became visible. But visibility is not causation. Dinner ended at eight-thirty. Routine resumed immediately afterward. 
That’s where the murder lives. The rope was theatre. The struggle was fiction. The body was staged because someone needed time—not strength. 
William drank the poison and lived. That tells us dosage. 
Rory planned a crime and failed. 
That tells us method. 
Adam and Sasha wanted money and lacked access and knowledge. 
That tells us motive without mechanism. 
Which leaves access without suspicion. Only one person in this house could make physical contact with Lord Murphy without alarm. Only one person had reason to study plants and reason not to smell them. Only one person vanished briefly and returned with nothing to show for it. Clever people don’t commit obvious murders. They create plausible accidents. And accidents,” she said softly, slowing down again, “require preparation.”
The silence that followed was heavy.
Paige looked directly at Amanda.
“The seeds are tiny, easily concealed” Paige looked directly at Amanda’s hairpin “When crushed they would become lethal if ingested”

Amanda answered without hesitation, her voice helpful and bright. “Of course it would. It’s simple chemistry.”

Paige smiled faintly.
“Interesting,” she said. “But what you didn't know is that it was only recently published that after a few hours, the crushed seeds give off a scent not unsimilar to cinnamon. You wouldn’t know this for two reasons. The first, the book that Claire gifted you was out of date, she has recently purchased a more up to date version which, among other things, speaks of the seeds cinnamon scent.”
Amanda smiled “And the second?”
“Your inability to smell, that cold of yours, dulled the senses”

The air went tight. The smile on Amanda’s face froze, not in fear, but in recognition. It was the look of a chess player realizing they had missed a pawn.
“I suspect, that it was no mere coincidence that your uncle fell ill and continued to be ill under your care." Paige said before pausing, “Having never handled this poison before, you would’ve needed a test subject”

Amanda laughed softly. It was a charming, bell-like sound.
“Oh, Professor,” she said, shaking her head. “You’re very clever. But clever isn’t the same as correct.”

She straightened, smoothing her skirt and clapped gently. She removed her hairpin, unscrewed the top, held it up to her nose and breathed deeply.
“Why yes, they do smell like cinnamon, my cold must be clearing up” she smiled, “However, you can’t prove I intended to kill grandfather, can you?. Perhaps I wanted to frighten him. Teach him a lesson for being so beastly to everyone. Perhaps it was reckless curiosity. Cinnamon was everywhere, after all. Who’s to say I didn’t drop a seed or two by mistake? Or perhaps I was just... clumsy.”

She spread her hands wide.
“An accident?”
"A prank?"
"A lesson?"
"So many legal avenues to explore"

Paige regarded her for a long moment. The room waited for the explosion, for the accusation, for the handcuffs.
Then, quietly:
“Intent is not something we find in a body, Miss Smythe. It is something we infer from preparation.”
She exhaled, a long, weary sound.

“And inference is not proof, my dear Professor Turner.”
Amanda smiled—a small, victorious thing. She knew the law. She knew that without a confession, and most importantly without clear provable intent, she was untouchable.

Paige stepped aside, opening space rather than closing it. She walked to the door where Walter stood.
“You may walk free today,” she said evenly, looking back at the young woman. “No charges. No arrest.”

Amanda blinked, surprised. Even Adam looked confused.

“Or,” Paige continued, “you may explain why every coincidence in this house bends toward you. You may explain why the universe seems to conspire to place poisons in your proximity and victims in your path.”

The smile faltered—just a fraction.

Paige leaned in, her voice low, meant only for the silence of the room.
“Either way, you should understand something.”

She straightened, signaling Alfred to open the door.
“Patterns don’t forget.”

Amanda stood, walked towards Paige and simply smiled, then gently said 
“That was truly impressive Professor, better than I could’ve hoped, I do hope we meet again. By the way, ever wonder how my father died? Such a strange accident”, with that she walked away.

The cats rose from the rug and drifted toward Alfred, leaving the family alone in the drawing room. No one spoke.

Paige walked out into the hallway, Walter following close behind. She didn’t look back at the victory she had lost, or the truth she had found. She simply put on her gloves, ready for the London air.
“Come, Walter,” she said. “We have a botanist to retrieve from a pub.”
