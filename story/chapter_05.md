# Chapter 5 — A Mixture of Truth and Lies


Alfred led Paige and Walter toward the manor’s library—a richly decorated room adjacent to the crime scene itself. From the dining hall behind them, the Murphy siblings continued bickering loud enough to rattle glassware.
“The room beside the study,” Paige murmured, adjusting her cuffs. “Excellent. Proximity sharpens the nerves. Walter, by the door, if you please. Privacy encourages confession.”
Walter exchanged a look with Alfred; both braced themselves.

### Interview 1: William Murphy
William lurched into the library, clutching his cognac glass like a life preserver. Alcohol and a faint ribbon of cinnamon clung to him.
“Let’s get this over with, Professor,” he grumbled. “I’m not interested in academia. Just tell us when we get the money.”
“The money your father left to his cats?” Paige countered pleasantly. “Kal and Màni?”
William flinched.

 “A joke. He was being cruel. The Will must be missing—or tampered with. Either way, we inherit.”
“Of course. Now, the night of his death?”
William claimed he went to bed early, woke up later, wandered the grounds, and approached the study.
“I heard the door creak. I was going to confront him about those blasted cats, but… I didn’t. Didn’t see anyone. Went back to bed.”

Paige observed the trembling hands, the unfocused eyes, the inconsistencies.
“You heard the door, you were furious, you were close… yet saw nothing. Curious.”
She made a quiet note of the cinnamon lingering on him—far too prominent to dismiss.
“Thank you, William. Send in your brother.”

### Interview 2: Rory Murphy
Rory glided in with performative poise, lowering himself into the chair as though posing for a portrait. Another curl of illegal cigar smoke drifted off his hand.
“You don’t mind, do you, Professor? A small pleasure.”

“I mind the legality,” Paige replied. “That brand is banned in England. Trade disputes. Only one supplier.”
Rory offered a glib wave.

“Gifts, Professor. Admirers. I had one after Father’s little… announcement. Went straight to my balcony to cool off.”
 
“And did you see anything unusual?”
“Skulking,” Rory said immediately. “The groundskeeper, Julian. Moving near the hedges by the staff quarters. Not where he should have been.”
Paige filed the detail away. The speed of his answer was almost rehearsed.

### Interview 3: Sasha Smythe
Sasha entered like a woman arriving at her own eulogy: tragic, poised, and catastrophically dramatic.
Her perfume had strength without depth—old, stretched thin.

Paige’s eyes flicked to the shoes again. Scuffed, worn, desperate.
“This entire ordeal is giving me a dreadful headache,” Sasha sighed. “My father was awful, but one expects dignity in death.”
“The evening, Mrs. Smythe?”
“I retired early. A headache.”

She dabbed theatrically at her eyes. “I closed my window because Rory’s vile cigars were filling the room. After that, nothing. I woke before sunrise, as always.”
Paige offered no sympathy—only analysis.
“It must be difficult,” she said lightly, “managing grief and a shrinking income simultaneously.”
Sasha stiffened.
 “I trust the law will restore order—once that missing Will is found.”

### Interview 4: Adam Murphy 
Adam entered smelling faintly—just faintly—of cinnamon. Charming, warm, endlessly apologetic.
“Sorry about the chaos, Professor. Amanda and I were the last awake. Planning our next adventure. India, perhaps.”
“Later,” Paige thought. “First: truth.”
“And the end of the evening?” she asked.
“Amanda hugged Father, as usual. Then she and I went upstairs. She left me briefly—snack craving. Came back without food, so we talked about travel instead. Italy seems more reasonable.”
“And she was with you until midnight?”
“At least.”

### Interview 5: Amanda Smythe
Amanda swept in with bright eyes and a voice thick with congestion.
“If only I weren’t so bunged up,” she said. “I can barely smell a thing.”
Paige did not smile this time.
“Your uncle says you hugged your grandfather goodnight.”

“I did! I think I'm the only one in the family he tolerates”

Then, sweetly: “And yes, I went for a snack. But when I came back up, I thought I saw Grandfather slip into his study. It was dark, but… I think it was him.”
Perfectly placed. Perfectly convenient.
“Thank you, Miss Smythe,” Paige replied. “Very helpful.”
“Are you onto something?” Amanda asked eagerly.
“Speculation without evidence is noise,” Paige replied.
Amanda nodded as if she’d received a profound lesson.

### The Study 
Alfred returned as Amanda left.
“I’m ready, Alfred. Show me the study.”
He opened the door to the room next door — eerily untouched, everything meticulously arranged except for the safe yawning open like a pulled tooth.

Paige paused, taking in the space.
 “Lord Murphy was found in this chair?” she murmured, brushing her fingers along the tall, oxblood-red leather. “And the police believe Julian crept in and strangled him here?”
Her tone suggested she found the idea anatomically unconvincing.

“Alfred,” she said, “look carefully. Anything out of place — even the slightest irregularity.”
The former soldier performed a slow sweep of the room, eyes sharp as a bayonet.
“There,” he said at last, pointing to the bar cart. “The cognac. It’s wrong.”

Paige moved to it.
“How so?” Walter asked.
“My lord insisted that particular bottle always face inward,” Alfred explained. “He said it kept ‘temptation turned away’.”
He stepped closer. “Someone used it and put it back incorrectly.”
 
Paige lifted the bottle with a gloved hand.
A swirl of amber brandy.
A sharp, familiar alcoholic burn.

And beneath it —

Cinnamon. Strong. Deliberate. Wrong.
“Ah,” she whispered. “A contradiction in routine.”
Walter frowned. “So William was drinking in here?”
“Oh, unquestionably,” Paige murmured. “And in doing so he contaminated himself with the same scent now clinging to his siblings… Claire’s room… and the servant path.”
Walter hesitated. “So what does the scent mean?”

Paige turned, eyes bright with momentum.
“It means the rope is theatre. A distraction. Lord Murphy’s true cause of death lies inside him, not around his neck.”
She strode for the door.
“Alfred, the coroner’s name. Immediately.”
“And after that?” Walter asked, trotting to keep up.
“After that? We determine why at least two people lied to us — and why nearly everyone on this property reeks of cinnamon.”

She raised a brow. “New perfume, Walter?”

Alfred cleared his throat.
“Mrs Clarke made apple pie for the luncheon, Professor. She uses cinnamon.”
“That might explain the people,” Walter said helpfully.

Paige halted mid-stride.
“Then, Walter… you can ask Mrs Clarke how cinnamon travelled from her kitchen to Lord Murphy’s brandy bottle.”
She pushed open the door. “Alfred — arrange a coach for St Bart’s. Walter — talk to the cook and meet me there.”
Walter blinked. “Professor… who lied to you?”

Paige swung around.
“William, obviously.”
 A beat.
 
“And Rory.”

“Rory?” Alfred echoed.

Paige’s words accelerated into their usual rapid-fire brilliance.
“Rory claims he saw Julian skulking by the hedges near the study. Sasha told us she closed her window because of his cigar smoke — and that she awoke at sunrise. Sunrise rises in the east. The balconies face west.”
She pointed sharply toward the back of the manor.
“Therefore—”

Walter’s eyes widened. “Rory couldn’t have seen Julian from the opposite side of the house.”
“Exactly!” Paige said, practically glowing. “Gentlemen — begin.”

Alfred hurried out. Walter followed.

Paige lingered a moment — two sleek black cats winding themselves around her ankles like living shadows.
She knelt, stroking their heads.
“What did you see, hm?” she whispered.
“What happened to your father?”
The cats purred, soft and eerie.
Paige rose.
And the entire investigation pivoted.
