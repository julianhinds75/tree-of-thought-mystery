# Chapter 7 — The Soggy Biscuit
### Part I: Interrogating Sly Si
The office of Simon Coulson, Esq. was perched precariously above a grocer’s shop, smelling of wilted cabbage and old parchment. It was a room designed for hiding, not for law.
Simon Coulson, a man who looked like a startled worm in a cheap suit, was mid-sentence when his door didn't just open—it vanished behind the sheer physical mass of the people entering.
Paige walked in first, her presence cool and clinical. Behind her came Georgie D., his shoulders nearly scraping both sides of the doorframe. And finally, like a silent shadow of impending doom, Julian Kent stepped inside. The room, already cramped, suddenly felt like a tin can being crushed.

Simon nearly fainted, his spectacles sliding down his nose. "I—I have a 4:00 p.m. appointment! You can't just—"
"Mr. Coulson," Paige interrupted, placing a stack of papers on his desk with the precision of a guillotine blade. "Let us dispense with the theater. I have a report from St. Bart’s revealing that Lord Murphy was poisoned, a summary of Mr. Kent’s cleared name, and a very detailed list of Rory Murphy’s mounting gambling debts."

She leaned in, her grey eyes piercing. "You’ve been involved in a forgery for—what shall we call it?—a young aristocrat’s tantrum."
Simon sputtered, his face turning a sickly shade of grey. "I—that's libel! I am a respected—"

Georgie D. stepped forward, the floorboards groaning under his weight. He cracked his knuckles with a sound like a gunshot.
"Simon, mate," Georgie rumbled, his Cockney accent thick and menacing. "I'm sure you're a lovely fella. But I've met damp biscuits firmer than you. So ’ere’s the deal: talk t’ the Professor before I get bored. And trust me, you don't want me looking for ways to entertain myself in this little box of yours. Isn’t that right Mr Kent?"

Julian said nothing. He simply stood behind Simon's chair, staring at him, a stone monolith of a man, his shadow falling across the desk.

Simon broke instantly.

"It was Rory!" he wailed, burying his face in his hands. "He approached me two weeks ago. He wanted a forged Will—one naming him as the sole heir. He promised me a 'consulting fee' the moment the estate transferred."

"And the original Will?" Paige pressed.

"He needed to destroy it first! That was the plan. His... associates, Billy and Sal, were supposed to 'deal with' the old man afterward. Rory never mentioned poison! He told me it would look like a 'tragic fit of passion.' He said the girl—the botanist—would take the fall as naturally as rain falls from the clouds!"

Paige’s lip curled in distaste. "A convenient scapegoat. Classic." She paused, then asked one final, sharp question. "Did Rory ever mention Peru?"
Simon blinked, wiping sweat from his brow. "Good heavens, yes. He said he hated the place, too hot. Complained that his little niece pestered him about going again."

Paige froze. For a half-second, the gears of her mind locked into a new alignment.

Georgie patted Simon’s shoulder, a gesture that almost sent the solicitor to the floor. "Good lad. See? Weren't so hard."
Paige turned to Julian and Georgie. "Gentlemen… we return to The Raven. Someone else has a story to tell."

### Part II: The Raven's Secrets

At The Raven, the atmosphere was softer. Walter sat at a corner table with Claire Summers. She was trembling, clutching a shawl around her shoulders as if she were freezing in the summer heat. Lucy Barrows set a cup of tea in front of her without a word, a silent gesture of protection.
"You are not under suspicion here, Claire," Walter said gently. "You are safe."

The dam broke, and Claire began to cry, the truth finally spilling out. She confirmed what Julian had said: Lord Murphy had died in her room after she refused his advances. He had laughed at her, then began the violent, racking cough that ended in his collapse.
"But there's more," Claire whispered, wiping her eyes. "Rory... he visited me. More than once. After dark."

Walter stiffened. "Rory? What did he want?"
"He paid me for information. He wanted to know his father’s habits, which safe he used, whether the old man favored any particular child that week. He was obsessed with the Will."

"So Rory was scouting the territory," Walter noted, his pen flying across his notebook.

"The only one who was ever kind to me," Claire added softly, "was Miss Amanda. She brought me books. She sat with me when the others treated me like furniture. She treated me like a human being. She always provided a shoulder to cry on, especially when it came to her grandfather"
She looked up at Walter with a ghost of a smile. "She even asked me about my botany books. She said she liked the flowers. I thought nothing of it. I gave her a book as a gift."

The door to the pub swung open. Paige stepped in, her eyes catching Walter’s.
"Book?" Paige asked, her voice low and dangerous.

Claire nodded. "Yes, Professor. Just an old one."
“But you didn’t have missing books on your shelf” Paige pressed
“Oh, I got the latest edition, so I didn’t mind giving the old one away” Claire replied

Paige slowly sat at the table, her expression unreadable, though her eyes were moving a mile a minute. “Tell me, was it called Toxic and Poisonous Flora Across the Globe?” she finally said.
Stunned, Clair replied “Why, yes how did you know?”

"Walter," Claire said quietly, "the board is nearly complete. The 'kind' niece, the 'desperate' son, a murder plot, and a flower that smells like cinnamon."
She looked at Julian, then at Claire, and finally at Walter.
"Get some rest Walter, tomorrow, we return to Murphy Manor. It’s time to show the family exactly what happens when you invite a physicist to a murder."
