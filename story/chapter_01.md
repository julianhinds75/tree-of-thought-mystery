# The Cat That Got The Cream - A Paige Turner Mystery

## Chapter 1 — The Summons

King Winston University sprawled across the hillside like a small, self-important village. Its clock towers stabbed the sky; its long stone corridors echoed with the patter of hurried footsteps and the droning lectures of ancient dons. It was here, between the cloistered arches and ivory towers, that young Walter Bishop strode with purpose.
“Pardon—terribly sorry—my apologies—”
 Walter squeezed past clusters of gossiping undergraduates, dodged a trio of debating philosophers, and nearly collided with a stack of precariously balanced textbooks carried by a startled first-year.
 
He was stopped, as always, by Sebastian Wallace — a bespectacled, self-appointed guardian of Professor Paige Turner’s academic sanctity.
“Mr Bishop!” Sebastian squeaked, blocking his path like an overprotective terrier. “Whatever ‘little caper’ you intend to drag the Professor into, I must insist—”

“Sebastian,” Walter sighed, leaning around him, “this concerns a murder.”
“That is exactly what I’m talking about!”
After a brief comedic tangle — involving Walter’s shoulder, Sebastian’s stubborn stance, and a dropped notebook — Walter finally burst into the lecture hall.

Professor Paige Turner did not turn.

Her back was to the crowded room, chalk in hand, the blackboard behind her a battleground of equations, diagrams, and symbols that made Walter’s head throb. The students — thirty or more of them — leaned forward as she dismantled the long-term viability of fossil fuels.
“…and as you will observe,” Paige said, drawing a line through an elaborate formula, “the inevitable exhaustion of coal reserves forces us to seek renewable alternatives. Applied physics will do the heavy lifting in the next century, though Miss Hathaway”—she pointed her chalk like a rapier at a flustered student—“still insists coal is the most efficient fuel source.”
“It is—was—Professor,” Martha stammered.
“Was,” Paige corrected curtly. “Past tense. Much like your argument.”

Walter, propping his shoulder against the door to prevent Sebastian’s re-entry, cleared his throat.
“Professor Turner—may I—just a moment—”
Paige continued writing.
“Professor,” he tried again, louder this time. “It’s about the murder at Murphy Manor.”
Nothing.
“The police have arrested my client, Julian Kent.”

Still nothing.

Walter exhaled. He knew exactly how to pierce her academic armour.
“The police say,” he said, pitching his voice above the scratching chalk, “that Julian strangled Lord Murphy with a length of rope.”

Paige froze.

The chalk snapped in her fingers.
“Don’t be absurd, Walter,” she said without turning. “A heavily muscled man—well over six feet tall—blinded by jealous rage would hardly rummage for equipment to dispatch a seventy-seven-year-old with latent heart disease. He would use his hands.”
She clapped her notebook shut.

“Class dismissed. Read chapters fourteen through twenty-four. I expect reports on my desk by Monday.”
Sebastian took this moment to slip past Walter triumphantly, only for Paige to say, without looking:
Paige finally turned, her sharp grey eyes locking onto Walter.
 “Walter — or whoever that is attempting to hide behind you — fetch my coat.”
Walter blinked at the window.
 “Professor, it’s thirty-two degrees outside.”
She paused, looked out the window, and nodded.

 “Ah. So it is. No coat. Very well. Summon a coach — I wish to speak to Mr Kent before Inspector Jefferies ruins the entire case.”
Walter smirked at a deflated Sebastian as he and Paige swept down the hall.
Within minutes, they were outside, stepping into the first available carriage bound for Scotland Yard.
The hunt had begun.
