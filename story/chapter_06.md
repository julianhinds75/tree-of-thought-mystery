# Chapter 6 — Two Professional Opinions
### St Bart’s Hospital — Coroner’s Office
The corridors of the Coroner’s Office were sterile and quiet — a jarring contrast to the venomous chaos of Murphy Manor. Dr. Leslie Simmonds greeted Paige and Walter at reception. His suit was pressed so crisply it could have been ironed directly onto him. The file in his hands was arranged with obsessive mathematical precision.
“Professor Turner,” he said, adjusting his glasses. “As always, a pleasure. Though I do hope this case will not become yet another of your… algebraic reinterpretations of reality.”
“Nonsense, Doctor,” Paige replied. “The universe prefers elegance over simplicity — you know this.”

Her eyes drifted to the pocket chessboard on his desk.
“Your Queen is exposed to the Knight,” she said. “Checkmate in one.”
Dr. Simmonds blinked rapidly. “Impossible — you couldn’t see that from—”
“Physics, Doctor. Vector trajectory. Now, the body, if you please.”
Walter arrived just in time for Dr. Simmonds to lead them into the examination room.

The sheet was drawn back, revealing Lord Murphy’s pale, slack face. Paige leaned over him like a professor over a neglected dissertation.

“Aha!”

Both men jumped.
“Professor,” Walter hissed, “this is a morgue.”
Paige tapped the ligature mark.
 “Notice anything?”

Walter frowned. “It’s… straight?”
“Perfectly horizontal,” Paige said. “Which is impossible if Julian strangled Lord Murphy from behind the high-backed library chair.”

She traced the air, demonstrating the angle.
“If a tall man pulled a rope upward from behind a seated victim… the mark would be diagonal. Never horizontal.”
Dr. Simmonds nodded. “She’s correct. The staging is clumsy.”
Paige straightened. “Meaning Julian is innocent of strangulation. The rope is theatrics. The body was already dying — or dead — before the staging took place.”

### The Internal Anomaly
The cinnamon smell hit her again — stronger here, trapped by cold walls and metal instruments.
“What did you find internally?” Paige asked.

Dr. Simmonds opened the autopsy file with morbid pride.
“Inflamed lungs. Collapsed lung. Thickened saliva. Swollen tongue. Hallmarks of catastrophic respiratory failure. All symptoms that mimic cinnamon toxicity… without a visible trace of cinnamon.”
“That’s impossible,” Walter said.
“No,” Paige countered softly. “It’s botanical.”

She remembered the flecks of yellow on Claire’s nightgown.
“Doctor… did Lord Murphy cough violently before death?”
“Violently enough to rupture small vessels,” Simmonds confirmed.
Paige inhaled sharply. “Then he was near Claire when he began seizing. Those flecks were not pie residue — they were sputum.”

She glanced again at the file.
“What poison mimics cinnamon… but leaves no trace?”

Dr. Simmonds shrugged helplessly. “Ask a botanist. My business is death, not gardening.”
Walter’s eyes lit up. “Professor Wright.”
“Excellent,” Paige said. “The man of petals awaits.”

### King Winston University — Botanical Department
Professor Elijah Wright’s office looked like a greenhouse had collided with a library in a catastrophic romantic entanglement.
“Toxic plants!” he boomed when they entered. “Nature’s philosophers! Nothing teaches quite like agony.”
“We’re looking for one in particular,” Paige said. “Causes lung collapse, saliva thickening, cinnamon scent upon breakdown. Lethal. Rapid.”

Professor Wright froze mid-ramble.
“Oh. That one.”
He snatched a thick volume from a stack without looking.
“Toxic and Poisonous Flora Across the Globe. The new edition. The old one lacked the appendix.”

He flipped rapidly.
“Here! The Peruvian Cat’s Eye. Only catalogued last year.”
Paige stared at the elegant drawing — a pale flower with a glowing, feline pupil at its centre.
“When crushed,” Wright continued, “its seeds release compounds that leave a cinnamon-like aroma in the throat and lungs. Quite poetic, really, if you overlook the death.”

Paige absorbed the information, but for the first time that day, she did not let her conclusions surface.
Too many variables. Too many paths.

Claire kept a box of exotic seeds in her room.
Adam Murphy had travelled to Peru only recently.
Amanda Smythe wore a floral hairpin of identical design — but so did half the fashion plates in Paris.
And cinnamon clung to far too many people.
A single thread was never enough.

She closed the botanical volume and nodded once to Professor Wright.
“Thank you, Professor. This narrows our path—but does not end it.”
Walter leaned in, confused. “But Professor… surely this means—”
Paige lifted a finger.
“It means,” she said softly, “that someone had access to a very rare toxin whose symptoms masquerade as strangulation. But access, Walter, is not guilt.”

She paced toward the office door.
“Claire had the seeds. Adam travelled with her father to Peru. Amanda and Adam both carry the scent. The cook had cinnamon in the kitchen. Rory consorts with smugglers. And William drinks anything with a cork.”

She turned back, eyes glittering.
“Anyone with a motive and access could have weaponised that flower.”
Walter swallowed. “…so the field of suspects remains large.”
“Beautifully large,” Paige replied.

“Walter,” Paige said, suddenly crisp, “you’re to go directly to Scotland Yard. Julian Kent is innocent. He is to be released.”
Walter nodded. “Back to the manor?”
“No,” Paige said sharply. “Take him to The Raven. No one at the manor must know he’s free.”

Walter looked uneasy. “The Raven is full of ruffians”
“And potential clients for a brilliant law graduate like yourself,” Paige finished, a hint of a smile touching her lips. “The landlord, Georgie D., is an incomparable source of information. I still have an itch about that cigar Rory was smoking—the illegal one.”
“The illegal one?”
“Smuggled, Walter. And Georgie D. knows every unsavory transaction in the city. Rory’s sister mentioned he’s been keeping ‘questionable company.’ I want to know who is funding Rory’s legal woes and how those cigars entered the country.
“I’ll meet you there. The real motive lies not in the poison… but in the company it keeps.”


### The Raven Pub
Paige arrives first and waits at the bar for Walter and Julian, she is talking to Lucy Barrows, Georgie D’s long time girlfriend. Georgie D is outside overseeing a delivery of ale.
When they arrive, Julian is grateful to Paige but is still reluctant to talk about what happened that night. Walter explains that Inspector Jeffries was far from happy to let Julian walk free, at that point from behind them a gravely said “Cos he’s trying to send another innocent bloke off to the jug! Bloody Rozzers, bollocks to em!”. 

“Georgie! Language” Lucy said, she was talking to a large and heavily muscled man who came into the pub “Sorry luv” Georgie replied. “Blimey, look at the size of this bloke, I thought I was the only giant in London. What’s your name mate” Georgie was addressing Julian.

Julian outstretched his large hand and the two giants exchanged pleasantries, “Julian Kent, nice to meet Mr…?” Julian said.
“Georgie D, any friend of the Prof can call me Georgie, everyone it’s Mr D” Georgie replied smiling, “Hang on…Kent…you’re the bloke that was in the papers, the one that knocked off old Dirty Murphy” Georgie replied, “Don’t speak ill of the dead dear” Lucy interrupted.
“Professor Turner proved that he didn’t do it, in fact she proved that it was impossible. So they had to throw out his confession and gave him a fine for wasting police time” said Walter.

Georgie let out a laugh that was so powerful that every patron in the pub turned their heads. “Corse she did, Prof ere is bloody brilliant, oh sorry Lucy luv” Georgie finally spoke. “Did she tell you how she kept me out the jug?” Georgie asked.

Julian shook his head. Professor Turner began to speak “It was simple really, Inspector Jeffries wrongly arrested Georgie for his involvement in an art smuggling ring. But if they looked at the evidence, that was staring at them in the face, they would’ve seen that that break-ins couldn’t have been committed by a sinestral”
Julian knotted his brow, confused, “Sinistral?”
“Lefty” Georgie clarified “Southpaw all my life, Prof ere helped me out. Turned over a new leaf. Believe it or not Julian, I used to be involved in a life that was less than, how should I say?... reputable" Georgie winked.
“But now Georgie is a model citizen of our fair city,” Paige said.
“Too bloody right, sorry Lucy. Absolute gent, through and through! God save the Queen!” Georgie raised a nearby glass and all of the patrons followed suit and echoed him.

Georgie leant closer to the group “Anyway, what brings you to my fine establishment?” he said quietly.
“First of all,” Paige said “we needed somewhere without prying ears. Because I want Julian here, to tell us what actually happened that night”
Julian looked down at the floor. Georgie put his massive hand on Julian’s equally large shoulder, it was a fatherly gesture. “Listen mate, so you seem like a decent sort of bloke, not like some posh a…” Georgie fought back the expletive that he was about to utter when Lucy shot a look at him, “absolutely awful people” Georgie corrected himself “Don’t throw away your life for a lie, the Prof ere can think her way out of any problem”.
Lucy walked up behind Georgie and tried to wrap her hands around his huge frame, “He’s right Mr Kent, Professor Paige gave me back my loveable giant, she can help you out”

Julian sighed and relaxed a little. “Alright,” he said quietly “I’ll tell you”


### The Night of Lord Murphy’s Death. Julian's Account
“I finished supper, Mrs Clarke saved some extra apple pie for me. Then I went to bed, must’ve been around 10.30. I couldn’t tell you how long I was asleep for. But I was woken up by knocking on my door. Really frantic knocking”

“Claire I presume” Paige said.

Julian looked momentarily shocked “Y–es, when I opened the door she just held onto me. She was so scared, she wouldn’t stop crying. She wasn’t making any sense” he continued.

“What did she say Julian?” Claire gently pushed.
‘'He's dead’, over and over. She just kept repeating it. I gave a little shot of sherry, Alfred likes to reward me for my hardwork.”  Julian said.

“Sounds like a good bloke” Georgie said
Julian nodded and continued “She managed to gather her thoughts and she told me that Lord Murphy came to her quarters, as he often did” Julian’s jaw tensed, “And he…he” Julian hesitated

“We know why he would visit Claire, Julian, don’t concern yourself with the nasty details.” Paige said gently

Julian continued “Oh right, well she said that she was trying to refuse him, she was trying to tell him that she wasn’t prepared to give into him again. He grabbed her and she said that she told him to stop, that she was in…love”
“Wiv who?” Georgie asked

“With Julian, you dopey sod” Lucy said “Oh the poor girl. Good for her for standing up to him, sounds like a bloody pig” Lucy said. Georgie smiled

“I love her as well,” Julian said “We were going to leave together when she had finished her work at the manor. She said that when she told him about me, he started laughing…but then…” Julian trailed off, still a little unsure.

Paige leant in closer “Started coughing?” she said
Julian's head snapped round to meet her eyes “Yes, yes, she said that he was coughing badly, grabbing at his throat. Then he collapsed and the coughing stopped. Claire said that she tried to shake him, but he wasn’t moving. She listened to his heart but there was nothing.”
“Tell me,” Paige interrupted, “Did Claire mention any alcohol on Lord Murphy’s breath?”
“Yes, reeked of it but she said it smelled funny, not the usual brandy smell. She said it smelt a little bit like cinnamon”
“I KNEW IT!” Paige yelled, turning the heads of the patrones, Paige looked around, then spoke quietly again “I knew it, the poison was administered via that cognac! What happened next Julian?”
“Well, I convinced her to show me Lord Murphy because I thought that he might still be alive. So we went back to her quarters and there he was, lying on the floor. I listened for a heartbeat, nothing” Julian said “Claire was still in a bit of shock, so I wanted to get the body out of there. I knew that his family and the police would blame her and I couldn’t let that happen.” Julian clenched his fist.
“Easy lad” Georgie said calmly, “Don’t blame you for taking care of your girlfriend, I’d do the same if I was in your shoes!”

Julian sighed “Well, I figured that I could take the heat off of her by making it look like I did it. So I took Claire back to my quarters and told her to stay there until I came back. I grabbed some rope and tools from my cabinet and headed back to the body. I slipped the rope around his neck, pulled it good and tight so it looked like I strangled him.”
“Oh how romantic, bloody grim but romantic” Lucy said

Julian smiled a little smile with sadness in his eyes. “Then I carried him back to his study. I had to be careful, Alfred’s quarters isn’t too far from the study.”
“I found your footprints in the soil near the hedges outside of the study, one set looked like you waited for while. What happened” Paige asked.

“Yes, right, well I was about to go I saw that there was a couple of people in the study” Julian said.

“Let me guess, William and Rory Murphy?” Paige said. Before Julian could answer, Paige said “Rory claims that he saw you.”

“That bastard” Julian said, “Oh, so Miss Lucy” he said apologetically.

“That’s alright luv, you’re upset” Lucy said gently.

“What were the brothers Murphy doing?” Paige asked
“Well, William didn’t stay long, he just staggered to the booze, poured himself a glass and left. Rory was looking through the desk drawers, then he went over to the safe, looked like he was trying to pick it. He was getting annoyed and gave up, then he must’ve spotted me. Luckily I put Lord Murphy’s body on the ground before Rory saw him.” Julian continued
“What happened when Rory saw you Julian?” asked Walter , in a very confident lawyer tone.

“He looked like he got caught red-handed, he turned on his heel and left.” Julian said, “So I picked up the body and headed over to the study. I’ve worked there for years, it looks like a fortress but all the locks can be picked easy enough, I should know, I installed them”
“And when you entered the study?” Paige asked

“I made a mess, to make it look like we were fighting, then I put him in his chair, locked the study and went back to Claire.” Julian replied.
Georgie shook his head “No wonder Prof ere took on your case, look at the size of you! Whose gonna put up a fight against you? You silly sod” Georgie chuckled, “I knew you were innocent. Look, I’ve known killers, real nasty blokes, you ain’t got that look in yer eye Jules”
“Thanks Georgie” Julian said quietly, “When I got back I told Claire what I did and what to say when the police got there. That I followed Lord Murphy and killed him because I was mad with jealousy”

“Bet she weren’t happy about that” Lucy said whilst running a cloth over the bar.
Julian shook his head, “Not really no. But if I didn’t then the family would blame her. I won’t let her rot in a cell.” Julian’s fist clenched.

“Thank you Julian, you’ve helped fill in a lot of missing pieces” Paige said, “I need you to stay with friends or family for a little while. I need the Murphy family and yes even Claire, to continue thinking that you’re still in Scotland Yard”
Julian frowned a little, “I don’t have any family and all my mates took jobs overseas”.

“Well in that case, you’re staying here” Georgie said with a huge grin “We can make up the spare room for you”
“Absoloutely, I’ll make you a nice cup of tea and a proper bit of grub.” Lucy said

“I–I don’t know what to say.” Julian said gratefully “Thank you both. If you need any help here, I can do lots of different jobs”
“Good, cos this place is falling apart” Lucy said “Follow me, the living room upstairs could do with some paint, and when that’s done you can fix the backdoor” Lucy took Julian’s hand and led him to the back room.

“She’ll keep him working for years” Georgie chuckled, then he leant in closer “That Rory bloke that Jules saw in the study, is this him?” Georgie produced a recent copy of The Times, the headline read “LORD MURPHY MURDERED!”. Below the headline had the pictures of the Murphy children William, Adam, Sasha and Rory, only Amanda was missing.

“See Walter” Paige said, elbowing him, “Georgie is a veritable fountain of knowledge. Yes, that is indeed the, known, last born son of Lord Murphy. I take it that you have seen him before?” Said Paige
“A few times yeah,” Georgie chuckled again, “That’s why you’re here, not just for ol Jules. You’ve got your eyes on that little posh loud mouth”.

Walter looked at both of them, confused “Sorry Professor, but how did you know to speak to Georgie about Rory?”
“The cigars Walter, come now, keep up” Paige said, “Those awful things are illegal here and the only way to get them into the country is if they’re smuggled in.”

“And the most notorious smuggling operation is conducted by the Flowers gang, headed by Denny Flowers himself” Walter whispered.
“Unproven of course, Mr Flowers is a respected gent” Georgie said with a wink, “Some of his, less than respectable, associates have a lot of fingers in a lot of pies”
“And Rory has met some of these associates?” Paige asked.

Georgie laughed again, “Met? The way him and his little lawyer mate natter away in that corner over there, you’d think they grew up together”
Paige furrowed her eyebrows, “Lawyer friend? What lawyer friend?”
Walter raised his eyebrows, “You mean there’s something you don’t know?”

“Don’t be facetious Walter” Paige replied “Georgie, do you know which members of the Flowers gang they were talking to?”
Georgie thought for a moment “Billy Fingers and Sal the Knife, Italian bloke”

“A thief and a killer” Paige whispered, “Yes, oh that’s clever! Is that clever? Yes, it is. Oh how awful, but clever” Paige was muttering to herself.
Georgie and Walter looked at each other confused, “Um Professor” said Walter, “Care to tell us why, whatever it is and why it’s clever”
“Hm? Oh yes,” Paige snapped out of her thought process “Julian said that he saw Rory and William in the study and we know that the study remains locked. So how did Rory get in?”

“Well he…” Walter was about to speak until, 
“Shh, shh” Georgie interrupted, “I like it when she goes off on one like this”

Paige continued “He learned how to pick the lock, and how did he learn?”
“Ol Billy Fingers” Georgie said smiling, “Lucy said that she saw him with his toolkit last time they were together”
“But Mr Fingers is a master at lockpicking, but has never been successful with a safe has he?” Paige replied “And I would imagine that Billy made some quick cash from Rory by convincing the fool that his methods would work on any lock, Julian did say that Rory gave up trying to open the safe”

“Ah, yes” Walter understood “Billy wouldn’t fully trust someone like Rory and would want some payment up front, then he taught him enough to break into the study and if, by chance, Rory somehow got into the safe then Billy would get even more money” Walter said triumphantly
“Well done Walter” Paige said “Well done indeed. Now, how does the lawyer and the killer fit into your analysis?” 

Walter paused, thought hard “Well, um…why don’t you share your thoughts first and then we can compare notes afterwards” he said, hoping that his bluff would pay off.
Georgie smiled
“I would surmise that Rory wanted to get his hands on the Will and have it changed, perhaps with the help of the lawyer that Georgie spoke of, if successful he would have Sal the Knife kill Lord Murphy before the Will could be changed back.” Paige said.

Walter and Georgie nodded.
“But we don’t know that this other fellow is a lawyer, apart from Georgie’s somewhat vague description” Walter said, he glanced at Georgie “Um, no offence Georgie”
Georgie smiled, “None taken. He was an old bloke, carried around one those cases like you do Walter”

Paige thought for a moment before speaking “That’s a start. Walter correct me if I’m wrong, but when you’re in the profession for a number of years you are gifted a personalised briefcase? With your initials engraved near the clasp”
“Why yes, after 30 years or so” Walter replied

Paige smiled and turned to Georgie “Think hard, did our lawyer friend have his initials on his case?”
Georgie thought for a moment “Bloody hell, yeah he did, ‘S.C’, yeah, that was it ‘S.C.” 
“SPLENDID!” Paige yelled, “Well Walter? Who’s S.C.?

Walter looked stunned for a second, “Well, um, there’s, let me think…”
“In your own time Walter” Georgie said
“But quite quickly” Paige countered

Walter sighed “Sebastian Collins, no, he passed away last year”
“Are any of them alive and well?” Paige pressed

Walter looked flummoxed “Sorry, yes, Stephen Charlton, but he lives in India
