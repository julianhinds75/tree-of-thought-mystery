# Chapter 8 — The Pleiades Marbles
The click of the latch was the only sound as Paige Turner let herself into her Knightsbridge flat. The air here was scented with old paper, a balm of familiarity after the rank chaos of the day.
Isaac, her sleek black cat with a very distinguished looking white patch on his chest, greeted her with a demanding mewl, rubbing himself against the cuff of her tailored trousers.
"Patience, Isaac," Paige murmured, her voice losing the brittle, professional edge it carried outside. She slipped out of her jacket and kicked off her flat shoes, shedding the performance of Professor Turner. The rhythmic clatter of the kettle being filled and placed on the hob was a small, comforting ritual—a necessary transition from the world of human malice to the sanctuary of logic.

Paige walked to a low, ornate table and retrieved a velvet pouch. Inside were two dozen polished glass marbles in varying shades: clear, amber, deep red, and smoky grey. This was her internal map, a physical manifestation of her analytical method, her mind laid bare.
She poured a cup of tea, then knelt on the smooth wooden floor. She placed a single clear marble at the center of the space. This was Lord Murphy.

She then rolled the remaining marbles across the floor in a deliberate scatter. As if by an unseen force, they began to group themselves. Isaac batted a stray amber marble; Paige absently nudged it back into the growing pattern. This was how the Professor thought when the mask was off—in silence, with geometry.

Paige began assigning meaning, touching each cluster, confirming the facts:
The central group representing Lord Murphy was now surrounded by circumstantial details. She placed a smoky grey marble for Poisoned, another for Cinnamon scent, a dark amber one for Favourite cognac, and a chipped one for Routine violated and Death staged.
"Routine," Paige murmured, gently pushing the marbles back into alignment. The center held the entire truth, but the peripheral facts dictated the narrative.

She moved to the most dense grouping, the facts surrounding Amanda. This cluster was large but stubbornly refused to center itself on the victim. It contained marbles representing: Travelled to Peru, Cat’s Eye hairpin, Trusted enough to hug him, and Midnight absence, Claire’s book. 
“Dinner finished just after half past eight,” Paige murmured, rolling a blue marble toward another.

“And by a quarter past ten… he was always with Claire.”
Marbles for Cinnamon present and Cold (olfactory blind spot) sat beside one for Knowledge adjacent to botany. It was too large, too interconnected, but lacked the direct, causal line.
“They all think it happened late,” Paige said, watching Isaac bat a marble beneath the sofa.

“But it didn’t, did it?”
She isolated the facts related to William: Drank cognac, Present in study, and Cinnamon contamination. His marbles were few and positioned near the center, clearly having been exposed to the threat.
"Exposure," Paige stated, moving his cluster away slightly. "Not origin."

The cluster for Rory formed a neat, purposeful line: Criminal intent, Outside help (Billy/Sal), Peru-adjacent goods (the illegal cigars), and Knowledge seeking (his payments to Claire). His marbles represented a clear plan, detailed and linear, but not the murder itself, but an intended murder was clearly evident. He was planning a robbery and forgery.
Sasha’s facts—financial desperation—remained peripheral, untouched. Her motive was too broad, her access too limited, but it would be foolish to dismiss her as a suspect completely.

Paige stepped back, gazing at the complex web of glass. She saw clearly now: the cinnamon appeared only where proximity and access overlapped. Travel, access, trust, and timing all intersected at one singular point.
“This window,” she said softly, nudging one marble between two clusters.

“No one remembers this one.”
The pattern was not a linear chain of events but a constellation.
She knelt again. "Seven points," she murmured, tracing an imaginary line through the clusters. "But only one alignment."

She thought of the Pleiades, the star cluster known for its visible, bright seven stars, which often appear evenly distributed, but are in fact anchored by a common origin.
This was the moment of absolute certainty. The killer had disguised their actions using the sheer volume of facts surrounding others.
Paige did not smile. The pleasure of the solution was instantly replaced by the weight of the human cost.

She poured her second cup of tea, the steam warming her face. She watched Isaac curl up on her discarded jacket.
"Intent," she said softly into the silence. "Blast! There’s always something"

Clever people leave plausible exits, and she knew this case would not end neatly. The law required confession or irrefutable, physical proof. Paige had only the convergence of logic.

Paige gathered the marbles back into their velvet pouch—except one, the clear marble representing Lord Murphy, which she left out on the wooden floor. She did not name the killer.
She opened her notebook and wrote a single line, the mission statement for the final act:
“Confrontation must force choice, not confession.”
She stood, her posture snapping back to Professor Turner. Tomorrow, she would return to the manor, remove every plausible exit, create unbearable pressure, and let the truth surface itself.
